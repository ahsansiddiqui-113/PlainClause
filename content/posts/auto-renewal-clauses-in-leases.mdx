Many residential leases renew automatically unless you give written notice by a specific date. Miss that date and you can be bound to another full term, or converted to an expensive month-to-month arrangement.

## What an auto-renewal clause looks like

Here is a typical example:

> "This Lease shall automatically renew for successive terms of twelve (12) months unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal not less than sixty (60) days prior to the expiration of the then-current term."

In plain English: if you do nothing, you stay another year. To leave, your written notice must arrive at least 60 days before the lease ends.

## Three things to check

1. **How notice must be delivered.** Email may not count. Many leases require certified mail or hand delivery.
2. **How the deadline is counted.** Some leases count from the end date, others from the next rent due date.
3. **What renewal costs.** Look for rent increases or fees that apply on renewal.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a landlord renew my lease without telling me?
In many places, yes, if the lease says so. Some jurisdictions require the landlord to remind you before the deadline. Check your local tenant rules.

### What if I miss the notice deadline?
Ask your landlord in writing whether they will agree to end the lease. Some will, especially if they can re-rent quickly.
